Same road, different day

Comparing road-survey runs across years and across repeat passes, on a simulated survey program over eastern Kentucky's real road network.

A pavement survey vehicle reads the road in short segments, here every 26.4 ft (0.005 mi), and tags each one with an odometer distance and a GPS fix. Two questions come up whenever a road is surveyed again:

  • Has it changed since last year? Every year's readings have to land on one fixed grid along the road, though no two runs start in the same place, count the same distance or follow the same GPS track.
  • How repeatable is the measurement? The same stretch is driven several times, and the runs have to be lined up position by position before they can be compared.

Real survey data is proprietary, so this project simulates a five-year survey program instead: two survey vehicles over the 1,861 miles of US and KY routes in the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet's District 12, on KYTC's public road network. The road surface is synthetic, a friction-like index from 0 to 100. That is what makes the exercise useful: the truth is known, so every step of the analysis can be scored against it.

What changed since the last survey?

Each road is drawn twice, one line per direction: the right-hand line for travel with the milepoints, the left-hand line against them. Color is the change in the reading since that direction's previous survey, smoothed over 0.1 mi. Resurfacing shows up as a rise of 8 points or more. Wear, the season and the two vehicles' small biases make the rest of the map shimmer. Pick a year, then click a road for its profile.

How well each step works

Each number compares the analysis with the simulator's truth, over 3.4 million segments from 2,430 runs.

Catching a miscalibrated odometer

Every run's odometer distance is compared with the length of its GPS track, and the median over a vehicle's runs that year estimates its odometer scale. In the simulation, SV2's odometer reads 0.5% short in 2023: enough to slide its readings one 26.4-ft cell per mile.

Lining up repeat runs

Six 3-mile reference sites are driven three times by each vehicle on one day each year. Runs are compared position by position, so first each run's segments are paired with the others'. The longest run's segments over the site are the stations. The methods differ in how they find another run's segment at each station:

  • Odometer: cut every run at the site's start, then pair segment by segment. Exact only while the odometers agree.
  • GPS: the nearest segment along the road by GPS position, in surveyed order.
  • LRS measure: the same, by the measure each segment was matched to.
  • True position: the same, fed the simulator's truth: the best the segments allow.

Paired by

BRSD is the mean, over 264-ft blocks, of the between-run standard deviation of the runs' block means. ICC(2,k) and ICC(3,k) come from a two-way ANOVA on the positions-by-runs table. (2,k) holds a run that reads high throughout against agreement and (3,k) doesn't, so the vehicles' biases show as the gap between them.

How it works

  1. Network. KYTC's All Roads layer for the seven District 12 counties, US and KY routes. Each feature's measures start from zero, so they're anchored to its begin and end milepoints. The -010 twin carriageways of divided highways are digitized in their direction of travel and are turned to run with the mainline's measure.
  2. Truth. Pavement sections about 1.2 mi long, each with its own level; wear in each direction every year; resurfacing now and then (6% of sections a year); texture that wanders along the road; and each cell's own local feature.
  3. Simulation. Routes chain across county lines into corridors, which the vehicles drive in runs of up to 30 mi. Built in on purpose: odometer error per vehicle-year and per run, GPS error that wanders and fixes that freeze, lane offset, slowdowns (readings under 15 mph are invalid), season, partial runs, re-drives and coverage gaps.
  4. Matching. Each segment's GPS track is smoothed, and every route within 150 ft is scored by distance and by how well its bearing matches the travel direction, with a penalty for leaving the route the run is confidently on. The segment's end points give its measure and direction. Where GPS fixes freeze, segments are placed by odometer distance between the matched segments on either side.
  5. Year-over-year grid. One cell per 0.005 mi of measure, with a slot for each direction of travel. A divided highway's far carriageway is paired to the nearest mainline cell. When a year covers a cell twice, the latest run wins; within a run, the reading nearest the cell's center wins.
  6. Change detection. Each cell's difference from its previous survey, as a 0.1-mi rolling median; 8 points or more flags resurfacing.
  7. Odometer check. Odometer distance over GPS track length per run, as a median per vehicle-year; flagged beyond 0.3%.
  8. Repeatability. Pairing as above, then BRSD and ICC per site, year and set of runs.

Data and code

Road network: Kentucky Transportation Cabinet, All Roads (public ArcGIS FeatureServer), fetched 2026-10-30. Everything measured on it is simulated; no real survey data is used. This project isn't affiliated with KYTC.

Code: github.com/ericallanwest/survey-runs (Python: NumPy, pandas, SciPy, Shapely, pyproj, PyArrow; MapLibre on this page). To rebuild everything: uv run survey-runs network, then simulate, analyze, repeat and site.
